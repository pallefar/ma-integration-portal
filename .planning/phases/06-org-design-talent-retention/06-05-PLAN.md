<objective>
Deliver ORG-03's register: a key-talent flight-risk entry carrying risk level, owner and mitigation,
readable only by a caller cleared for the `hr` area, writable only by the Integration Lead or admin,
and — the genuinely new control in this phase — **audited on successful read as well as on denial**.

Purpose: ORG-03's wording is stronger than DD-04's. "Access to it audited" means who *looked at* the
retention list, which is the actual MNPI compliance question. This server has no read-side audit path
anywhere today: not one of its GET handlers records a security event. The storage layer needs no
change to support it — the audit table's before/after columns are nullable — but the call site is new
code with no precedent to copy.

Output: three restricted routes, both halves of the D-03 audit, and a register surface whose entry
form does not exist in the DOM for a cleared member who is not the IL.

**Known, accepted labelling gap — recorded, not fixed (06-RESEARCH.md Assumptions Log A2).**
`recordSecurityEvent()` hardcodes its database-kind column to the control-plane value for every
caller. A flight-risk read is conceptually a deal-scoped event and will be recorded with the
control-plane label. Nothing filters or facets on that column today, so this is a labelling
inaccuracy rather than a functional gap, and fixing it would mean adding a parameter and threading it
through every existing call site — out of this phase's scope. Recorded here so it is not rediscovered
later as a surprise.

**Accepted cost of D-03, made explicit for the executor.** The decision deliberately rejected
session-scoped dedupe, so every successful read of the register writes a row. The register's own page
visit is therefore the only thing that may trigger this read — no background poll, no deal-home
refresh, no periodic revalidation. Plan 07's summary route reads pre-aggregated counts and is a
materially different disclosure; it must write NO audit row, and this plan's test asserts that
boundary in advance.

**Planner resolution of an internal UI-SPEC inconsistency, surfaced.** §Surface Contract 6 describes
the entry form's mitigation field as a required textarea, while the `partial | flight-risk-form` row
of §UI Considerations states mitigation is optional so an unmitigated entry is representable — and
D-22's owner nudge exists precisely to chase *unmitigated* high and critical entries. These cannot
both ship. **Resolved as optional**, because the nudge and the restricted-tier rollup both require an
unmitigated state to exist; person, risk level and owner remain required.

**Planner discretion recorded (delegated by 06-CONTEXT.md — collection naming and field set):** the
collection is `orgFlightRisks`, registered in Plan 01, and an entry is
`{id, employeeId, riskLevel, owner, mitigation, notes, linkedTaskIds, linkedRaidIds, createdAt}`. The
two link arrays are declared here, empty, so Plan 06's conversion has a stable bidirectional shape to
push into rather than inventing the field on first use.

Canon-referral note (prohibition probe): injection through `mitigation`/`owner`/`notes`, and
retention/erasure obligations over a register naming individuals, are canon — covered by VAL-02, the
`textContent` render discipline and `/gsd-secure-phase`. Not minted as bespoke prohibitions. The one
prohibition this plan carries is bespoke: this codebase happens to store engagement scores on the
exact `employees` rows the register links to, which is a fairness hazard no generic tool would flag.
</objective>
